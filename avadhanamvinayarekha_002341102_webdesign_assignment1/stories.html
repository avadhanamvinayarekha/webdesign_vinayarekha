Stories of Hope

These are simple, true-to-life stories to show how blood donation changes lives.

Naresh, 30

“Blood donors stood by me when I needed them most.”

Naresh is a 30-year-old who was always busy with work. One day he started feeling unusual symptoms and went for a general blood test. The results showed he had cancer. During treatment, he needed blood support from the blood bank.

Thanks to many donors, Naresh finished treatment and recovered. Today he is healthy again. Most medical rules don’t allow people who had cancer to donate blood afterward, so Naresh gives back in other ways—he volunteers, shares his story, and encourages healthy friends to donate.

Dinesh, 45 — and his friend Ganesh

“A friend’s donation can be the reason you see tomorrow.”

Dinesh was in a major accident and lost a lot of blood. Even after recovery, he still needs blood every month to stay well. The blood bank makes this possible for him.

Watching his friend struggle, Ganesh decided to help. He began donating regularly, knowing his blood can support Dinesh and others like him. Their friendship shows how powerful a single decision to donate can be.

Good to know

  • Most guidelines say people who have had cancer are not eligible to donate blood later. They can still support drives and spread awareness.
  • Healthy adults can often donate whole blood about every 8–12 weeks (local rules vary). Always check your local blood center’s eligibility page.
